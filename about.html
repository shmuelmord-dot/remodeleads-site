About

We only make money when you get a real appointment

That sentence isn't a slogan we put on the website. It's the entire structure of the business, and every other decision here follows from it.

Why we built it this way

The lead business is set up to be easy for the seller

Someone runs an ad. A homeowner fills in a form. That form gets sold to five contractors — and everything after it becomes your problem.

The calling. The qualifying. The scheduling. The forty-minute drive. All of it lands on you, after the seller has already been paid. Their cost was the ad. Their revenue was the same form, five times over.

Whether the homeowner actually owns the house, whether the "kitchen remodel" is a faucet swap, whether anyone ever picks up the phone — none of it changes what the lead seller made that day. It changes everything about what you made.

What that model actually costs you

  • Five to ten follow-up calls per lead, squeezed between real work
  • A ninety-second race against four contractors who bought the same form
  • A week of qualifying that ends at a renter or a handyman job
  • A no-show at the end of a long drive
  • An invoice that shows up either way
So we inverted it. We do the advertising. We make the calls. We do the qualifying and we do the scheduling. Then we get paid — at the end, once a verified homeowner is sitting on your calendar at a time they agreed to. If that never happens, we don't get paid. That isn't a guarantee we bolted on to make the pitch sound better. It's the shape of the company.

The why

We didn't get into advertising because we loved advertising. We got into it because of what advertising had already done to contractors.

Walk into any remodeling company in Southern California and ask the owner about marketing. You'll get a graveyard. The SEO company that took $2,500 a month for eleven months and delivered a ranking report. The lead vendor whose "exclusive" lead had already been sold to six other guys that morning. The agency that swore the phone would ring by spring, then stopped answering their own.

These are men who can frame a house, read a set of plans, run a crew of fourteen and finish a kitchen a week early. And the industry that sells them customers treats them like marks.

The part that always got us wasn't the money. It was the shrug. Contractors have been burned so many times they've stopped expecting anything else. That's not a market. That's a wound.

We built RemodeLeads to be the opposite of everything in that graveyard.

Why it can't work the old way

Here's the thing nobody in this industry will say out loud: the lying isn't a character problem. It's a structural one.

A marketing agency gets paid on the first of the month. Whether your phone rings or not. Whether the homeowner was qualified or not. Whether you closed a single job or not. Once that retainer clears, their incentive and your incentive are pointing in opposite directions. Everything after that — the dashboards, the "impressions," the quarterly strategy call — is just what a business does when it has already been paid.

So we removed the retainer. Entirely.

We don't sell traffic. We don't sell clicks or impressions or "brand awareness." We sell one thing: a homeowner, qualified through four checks, sitting in their own living room at a set time, both decision-makers present, ready to talk about the project they already told us they want done.

We get paid on the appointment. If we don't produce appointments, we don't eat. That's not a nice value we put on a wall. That's the only arrangement we were willing to build a company around.

The uncomfortable part, first

Most companies save the bad news for the fine print. We lead with it. Before you spend a dollar with us, you'll hear all of it:

There's no contract.

Nothing locks you in. If the appointments are good, you'll stay because they're good. If they're not, you should leave — and we'd rather you leave fast than resent us slowly.

You'll know what counts as an appointment before the first one is set.

We can't unbook a homeowner's Tuesday evening, and we don't run a business where the way you get made whole is by arguing. So the definition is settled up front, in writing, while it still costs nobody anything.

Not every appointment becomes a job.

We control who's sitting in that living room, how qualified they are, and whether both decision-makers are there when you arrive. We don't control what happens in the sixty minutes after that. Anyone who tells you they can close for you is selling you something.

We say these out loud, on purpose, before you pay. Because every hard truth we hand you up front is one less thing you have to discover later — and because after years of being told what they wanted to hear, most contractors have never had anyone in marketing just lay out the deal as it actually is.

That's the whole brand. Not "results-driven." Not "your growth partner." Just: we told you the truth first, and then we did what we said.

He makes it anyway, because the fastest way to become the next name in a contractor's graveyard is to take money from someone you already knew you couldn't help.

Neither of us came up in advertising. We came up building this — on the phone, on our own dime, learning what a real appointment looks like by producing a lot of bad ones first. Everything we know about this industry, we know because it cost us something.

How we operate

The principles we run on

Not values on a wall. Every one of these costs us something — which is the only reason to believe we actually follow them.

Human verification, not form checkboxes

A tick box on a landing page tells you nothing — anyone will click anything to see the next page. A person on the phone tells you whether they own the house, what the project really is, and whether they're serious. So a real person makes that call. Every time.

Appointments, not leads

A name and a number is not a lead. It's homework you didn't ask for. We don't consider our job finished until a homeowner has agreed to a specific day at a specific time. Anything short of that, we haven't delivered and we don't bill.

Your time slot is exclusive

We never send two contractors to the same homeowner at the same time. A homeowner is free to go get other quotes on their own — that's their house and their right. What we promise is that we aren't the ones handing you to your competitor.

Territories get capped, not oversold

We limit how many contractors we run in each county. When one fills, it goes to a waitlist instead of getting stuffed. That turns away revenue on purpose. The alternative is everybody in that county getting thinner appointments, including you.

The deal is settled before you spend anything

What counts as a delivered appointment is agreed in writing on the first call, while it still costs nobody anything. We don't run a business where the way you get made whole is by arguing — so we make sure there's nothing left to argue about.

We'd rather turn you away than take money we can't earn

If your crew cannot attend the volume you ask for, the campaigns never get enough signal to optimize and the appointments pile up unattended. If you don't clear those lines we'll say so on the call and point you at a lower daily cap — or at nothing, if that's the truthful answer.
