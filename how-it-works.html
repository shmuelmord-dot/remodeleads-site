How it works

From ad click to a name on your calendar

Six stages sit between a homeowner seeing an ad and your rep knocking on their door. We do five of them. Here's exactly what happens in each one — including the parts where we throw work away.

The delivery pipeline

What actually happens, step by step

No black box. This is the whole process, in order, with who owns each piece.

1
We handle it

We build and launch the campaigns

We write the ads and build the creative around kitchen and bath remodels specifically — not vague "home improvement" traffic that drags in gutter cleaning and window quotes. Targeting is set at the county level and then narrowed to the ZIP codes you're actually willing to drive to. We fund that spend ourselves — you never put money into an ad account, and you're billed $250 per appointment, not per click.

2
We handle it

A homeowner responds to the ad

They're interested enough to leave their name and number. That's all it means. At this point we don't know if they own the house, whether the "kitchen remodel" is a full gut or a new faucet, or whether they're eighteen months away from doing anything about it.

Nothing gets sent to you at this stage. This is the raw material that the rest of the industry sells as a lead.

3
We handle it

Our in-house call center calls them — a real person

Not a chatbot, not an SMS sequence. We run our own call centre of more than 20 agents, and one of them picks up the phone — usually within minutes, while the ad is still fresh in their mind. Speed matters here: the difference between calling in five minutes and calling in five hours is enormous.

On that call we verify four things. That they legally own the property. What the project scope genuinely is. That they intend to start within 30 to 90 days. And that every decision-maker will be present at the consultation. Fail any one of them and the call ends there.

4
We handle it

We book an exact date and time

Not "sometime next week." A specific day, a specific hour, agreed out loud by the homeowner on that same call while we have them on the line.

We book it against the availability you keep in the app and the travel radius you set. If your week is already at your cap, or the address sits outside the ZIPs you chose, we don't force it onto your calendar. A badly placed appointment costs you an hour of driving and costs us a contractor who stops trusting the calendar — neither of us wants it.

5
We handle it

It lands in the RemodeLeads contractor app

The appointment appears on your schedule with the homeowner's name, phone number, the exact street address, notes on the scope from the qualifying call, and the confirmed time.

No spreadsheet to check. No forwarded email. No "our office will call you with the details." It's there the moment it's booked, and your whole week is on one screen.

6
Your job

You show up and sell

That's the entire ask. Arrive on time, measure, present, quote, close.

Everything ahead of this point is our problem. This part is the part we genuinely cannot do for you — and it's the part that decides whether any of the rest of it was worth the money.

The filter

What never makes it to your calendar

Qualification only means something if we're willing to lose the appointment. These are the specific reasons a homeowner gets thanked and let go, after we've already paid to reach them.

Renters and tenants

They don't own the property, so they can't authorize the work. "I'll ask my landlord" and "my landlord might cover it" are the same answer: no appointment.

"Just getting ideas"

No start date, no urgency, browsing for inspiration. If they can't name a window inside the next 30 to 90 days, they're a future customer, not a consultation.

Handyman-size work

Vanity-only swaps, a leaking faucet, regrouting, a patch of drywall, one cabinet door. Real work for somebody — just not worth a 40-minute drive and a quote.

Won't commit to a time

"Call me back next week" isn't a booking. If they won't agree to a specific day and hour on the phone, they won't be home when your rep arrives either.

Only one of two decision-makers

A spouse or co-owner who "will look at it later" kills the close. If both can't be in the room, we move the appointment until they can — or we don't book it.

Outside your radius

A qualified homeowner in a ZIP you didn't pick is still a bad appointment for you. We either hold it for a contractor who covers that area, or we let it go.

The honest downside: this makes you fewer appointments. Every rejection above is money we already spent on advertising, written off. We could hand you all of it and let you sort it out on your own time — that's the entire business model of shared lead platforms. We'd rather send you a smaller number you can trust than a bigger number you have to re-qualify yourself. If you want volume above all else, we're the wrong vendor.
Your controls

The dials stay in your hands

We decide which homeowners qualify. You decide how many you get, where they come from, and when the tap is open. All of it lives in the RemodeLeads contractor app.

  • Weekly volume cap — set the number of appointments you want per week. We don't book past it, even if the demand is there.
  • Instant pause and resume — crews buried, or a rep out sick? Hit pause. Nothing new gets booked until you switch it back on.
  • Geo-targeting by county and ZIP — take all of Orange County, or exactly the eleven ZIPs around your shop. Change it whenever your crews move.
  • Service area — pick the counties and ZIP codes you'll actually drive to, and change them whenever your crews move.
  • Full appointment detail — name, phone, exact address, scope notes and the confirmed time, plus every line item you've been billed for.

Your side of the deal

What we need from you

It's a short list, and none of it is difficult. But when one of these slips, the appointment dies and the money is gone — ours on the ad spend, yours on the fee.

We'd rather be blunt about this up front than have the first bad week feel like a surprise. Contractors who do these five things get noticeably better results from the same appointments.

What counts as a delivered appointment →
  • Show up on time. The homeowner blocked out their afternoon for a specific hour. Late is bad; no-show burns the appointment and the fee stays billable.
  • Confirm the appointment. A short call or text the day before dramatically cuts no-shows. We book it; a reminder from the contractor who's actually coming holds it.
  • Keep your availability current. We book against what's in the app. If it says Thursday is open and Thursday isn't open, we'll put a real homeowner in a slot you can't cover.
  • Send someone who can actually sell. The homeowner is expecting a presentation and a number. A measure-and-email-later visit wastes the one moment they were ready to buy.
  • Tell us fast when something's wrong. Wrong address, renter, scope nothing like the notes? Flag it in the app that day. We listen back to the call and fix the script — but only if we hear about it.
Getting started

What the first 30 days actually look like

Nobody flips a switch and gets ten consultations on Monday. Here's the real shape of it, including the slow part.

1
Day 1

The onboarding call — about 15 minutes

We set your county and ZIPs, your weekly cap, the days and hours you can take consultations, and which track you're on. We also tell you on that call if your territory is already at capacity, rather than taking your money and figuring it out later.

2
Days 1–5

We build the campaigns

Ad creative, copy, targeting and the qualifying script — tuned to the work you actually want, whether that's full kitchens, master baths or whole-home jobs. Your app account is set up in the same window so the first appointment has somewhere to land. There's no setup fee for any of this.

3
Week 1–2

Campaigns go live and start learning

This is the slow stretch, and we're not going to dress it up. A brand-new campaign doesn't know who converts yet, so the first days cost more per booked consultation and produce fewer of them. It sharpens as real data comes in. If someone promises you a full pipeline on day three, they're guessing.

4
Week 2–3

The first appointments land

They start appearing in the app, usually a few at a time rather than all at once. Go to these yourself if you can. What you tell us after the first handful — scope was off, the ZIP was too far, the budget conversation went sideways — is what we tune the script and targeting on.

5
Week 4+

Volume settles toward your cap

Once the campaigns have learned and the script is tuned to your territory, weekly volume gets predictable and you can staff against it. That's the point where you raise the cap, add ZIPs, or — if it isn't working for you — stop. There's no contract holding you in either direction.

Two things that genuinely move this timeline. A tight territory with enough population, and a fast answer from you during setup. The slowest launches we see aren't campaign problems — they're a week lost waiting on availability, ZIPs or a card on file. Get those to us on the onboarding call and you'll be live inside the first week.

Want to see it run in your county?

Fifteen minutes. We'll walk your territory and ZIPs, tell you what capacity looks like there right now, and give you an honest read on what your first month would produce. If we can't cover your area — or you're not set up to work these appointments — we'll tell you that instead.
