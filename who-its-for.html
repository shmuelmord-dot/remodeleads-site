Fit

We're not for everyone. On purpose.

Most companies selling you appointments will take your money and figure out the fit later. We'd rather sort it out in fifteen minutes on the phone. Below is the honest version of who this works for — and who it doesn't. If you're in the second group, we'll say so on the call instead of billing you for six weeks first.

The short answer

Read both columns. Be honest with yourself.

Nobody wins when a contractor signs up out of optimism. If you see yourself on the right-hand side, we're not the right spend for you right now.

This works for you

If most of these describe your company, the model does its job.

  • Established kitchen & bath remodelers — you've done this for years and have work to show
  • General contractors doing full-home renovations and major remodel work
  • You have salespeople or estimators who can present and quote in the homeowner's kitchen
  • You show up when you said you would. Tuesday 2:00 means Tuesday 2:00
  • You have real capacity — crews and calendar room to actually start the work you sell
  • You want control of your ad spend without handing an agency a monthly retainer

This won't work for you

No judgment on the work itself. The economics just don't line up.

  • Handyman services — the ticket size can't carry an appointment fee
  • Painters only. We don't run paint campaigns and we'd be guessing
  • Small repair work — patch jobs, fixture swaps, service calls
  • You can't commit to a scheduled time. A missed appointment still bills, and the homeowner remembers
  • Nobody to run the consultation. If you're framing all day, the appointments go unattended
  • No capacity to attend the appointments — booked consultations that sit unworked cost you money and make us look bad
  • You want cheap leads to haggle over. We sell booked consultations at a fixed price, not a marketplace
One item on the right side isn't automatically a no. A good remodeler between salespeople is a timing problem, not a fit problem. A handyman business is a fit problem. Tell us which one you are on the call and we'll be straight with you about it.
Your profile

Find yourself here, then pick the track

Same appointments, same vetting, same exclusivity on both tracks. The only thing that changes is who funds the advertising — and that should follow your crew size, not your ego.

Solo operator or small crew

One to three people. You run the jobs and you do the selling yourself — usually at night, after the site work is done.

Basic · $250 per appointment

Start small and prove it. Set a low daily cap, take two or three consultations a week, and see what your close rate actually is before you widen the tap. There's no minimum holding you to anything.

The honest risk: every appointment is an evening you're not bidding something else. If your week is already full, wait until it isn't.

Growing shop, 3–8 people

You've got one or two people who sell, crews running, and you're trying to keep the pipeline from going quiet between jobs.

Confirmation + Insurance · $350

This is where confirmation pays. With two people selling, a no-show costs you a slot you could have filled. On the $350 plan a cancelled or missed meeting comes back as a credit, so you turn volume up without eating the misses.

Raise the daily cap when crews free up and pull it back when the calendar is packed. Most contractors live here for a long time.

Established company, 3+ salespeople

A real sales team, a dispatcher or office manager, and the crews to absorb ten or more consultations a week without dropping any.

Confirmation + filters

Buy the certainty. We fund the ad account, so a bad campaign month is our loss instead of your invoice. One line in your P&L: appointments delivered × your rate. No ad account to babysit, no spend forecast to argue about.

At this size the optional filters earn their keep too — kitchen-only, luxury, single-family. See the full pricing →

Project types

What we book — and what we screen out

Our call center asks about scope before anything gets on your calendar. These are the actual lines we draw.

We book these

  • Full kitchen remodels
  • Full bathroom remodels
  • Kitchen and bath combinations
  • Whole-home renovations
  • Major additions and structural remodels

We screen these out

  • Vanity-only swaps
  • Faucet and plumbing repairs
  • Countertop-only replacements
  • Interior or exterior painting
  • Flooring-only installs
  • Handyman punch lists
Why the line is where it is. A booked consultation costs $250 before you even get in the truck — then add your drive, an hour in someone's kitchen, and the estimate you write that evening. On a full kitchen remodel that cost disappears into the margin. On a countertop swap it is the margin, and you'd be paying us to lose money. Small jobs are real work and somebody should do them profitably — just not through a pre-booked appointment model. So we screen them out on the phone rather than letting them reach your calendar.

Not yet

If you're not a fit today

That's a normal place to be, and it's usually temporary. Here's what actually moves you into range — none of it requires spending a dollar with us.

Get one person selling

The single biggest unlock is one person whose job is to be in a homeowner's kitchen on a Tuesday afternoon. It doesn't have to be a hire — plenty of owners solve this by pulling themselves off the tools two days a week.

Move your average job up

If most of your revenue is repairs and small jobs, start quoting full baths and kitchens off the work you're already doing. Build the portfolio first. The appointment math only works once the tickets are big enough to carry it.

Then come back

No waitlist fee, no drip campaign, no salesperson calling you every quarter. Call us when something changed. The only caveat is territory — counties do fill, so check availability before you count on it.

Somewhere in between and genuinely unsure? Call (323) 800-8381 or email info@remodeleads.com. We'd rather spend fifteen minutes telling you "not yet" than take a payment we can't earn.

Territory

Being a fit also means being in range

We work six Southern California counties and cap how many contractors we run in each one. When a territory fills, it goes to a waitlist instead of getting oversold — because the whole point is that the homeowner in front of you isn't also sitting with three of your competitors.

Right trade, wrong county? Tell us where you work — we open new territories based on contractor demand, not on a map we drew in advance.

Still not sure you're a fit?

Good — that's exactly what the call is for. Fifteen minutes, your crew size, your county and what you actually build. We'll tell you which track fits, whether your territory is open, or that you should come back in six months. All three are real answers.
