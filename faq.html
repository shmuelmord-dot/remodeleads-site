Quality

Appointment quality & vetting

What we screen for, how a person confirms it, and what we flatly won't promise.

A person on our team calls and asks. Four things have to be true: they legally own the property, they intend to start within 30 to 90 days, the project is real scope — full kitchen, full bath, major renovation — and it isn't handyman work. Fail any one of them and the appointment never gets booked. These are questions on a recorded phone call, not checkboxes on a form.

Owner verified by phone, project inside the scope we agreed, intent to start within 30 to 90 days, every decision-maker present, an exact time they said yes to out loud, and an address inside the ZIP codes you picked. All six are confirmed by a person before it reaches your calendar, and the definition is settled in writing before the first appointment is ever booked.

The slot is yours. We never book two contractors onto the same homeowner, and we cap how many contractors we run per county so we're not selling the same territory five times over. What we can't control is a homeowner calling someone else on their own — plenty do. What we guarantee is that it wasn't us who sent them.

Some of them are, and we're not going to pretend we filter that out. Comparing two or three bids is normal behavior in this trade. What we do filter is people who aren't ready to start, don't own the place, or want a $900 job. If you're being compared, you're being compared on a real project with a real timeline — that's a bid you can win.

No. Anyone who guarantees you closed deals is lying to you. We guarantee a qualified homeowner at a confirmed address at a confirmed time. Closing is your job — your pricing, your presentation, your follow-up. If your close rate is the problem, more appointments won't fix it, and we'd rather say that now than after you've spent money with us.

No-shows happen, and we're not going to invent a percentage for a marketing page. We confirm with the homeowner ahead of the visit specifically to keep it low.

If you drive out and nobody's there, flag it in the app with your notes. A person on our team reads it and listens back to the call. That's how the script gets fixed, so the same thing doesn't happen to the next contractor in your county.

On Confirmation + Insurance you also get that appointment back as a free credit, applied automatically — there is nothing to claim. On the Basic plan a cancellation or no-show is not credited, which is the main reason most contractors take the confirmation plan.

We ask on the qualifying call who signs off on the project, and we book a time when everyone with a say can actually be there. "I need to talk to my wife" is the most common way a good consultation dies. If only one of them can make the slot, we'd rather move the appointment than burn your drive on a meeting that can't end in a decision.
